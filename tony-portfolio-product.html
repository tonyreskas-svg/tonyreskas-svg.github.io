Three pieces of work that show how I deliver

Each one started with a business need, went through requirements and a delivery plan, and ended in a process or tool people use every day.

ERP as the backbone of servicing

The need
Servicing needed parts, product, purchasing and cost data that Operations and Finance could both trust.
What I did
Own the Oracle NetSuite processes: new parts and product setup, BOMs, purchase orders, inventory and cost tracking, plus integrations and reports linking ERP data to operational dashboards.
Outcome
Consumption spend under control, supporting a ~20% reduction in spare parts cost.

The Technical Toolbox programme

The need
Field technicians across two regions had no consistent diagnostic platform or knowledge at the point of need.
What I did
Gathered requirements and acceptance criteria with technicians, engineers and the product team, then ran delivery in Scrum sprints with 3 software and 3 servicing engineers, including API integrations.
Outcome
Delivered in ~12 months and rolled out to 100+ technicians across the US and EU.

An operating model rolled out across regions

The need
The function had data in several systems but no shared way to decide where effort and investment should go.
What I did
Designed a six-pillar operating model with clear metrics, aligned Engineering, Product and Finance behind it, and set the governance rhythm with the SVP of Operations.
Outcome
Adopted across US and EU teams as the basis for how the function prioritises.

The metrics framework I built

The operating model I built: six pillars, each with a north star and the supporting metrics that explain it. It is how priorities, capacity and investment are argued at SLT level. Highlighted metrics are the cost, capacity and planning measures that rely on ERP and delivery data.

PillarNorth starSupporting metrics
Operational excellenceRefurbishment cycle time, SBD rateRefurbishment throughput vs target, service buffer coverage, on-time deployment rate, active fleet %, labour hours per refurb
Reliability engineeringMean distance between failures, first-time fix rateMTTR and repeat failures, preventive maintenance failure rate, recurrence of known faults
Financial efficiencyCost per refurb unit, spare parts costParts cost as % of refurb cost, swap time and unit cost vs repair
Tooling and dataReporting coverage, dashboard adoptionKPI dashboards with live data feeds, field data capture rate, Jira ticket-to-resolution cycle time, report delivery vs SLA
Strategic scalingFleet uptime, scaling milestone hit rateUS servicing model defined and validated, hub-and-spoke network efficiency, EU/US capacity vs deployment demand
Cross-functional integrationFeedback loop velocity, ops alignmentJoint OKR coverage, observation-to-change time, parts availability vs demand, field insights actioned per quarter

How I'd run product change and readiness

My approach to defining products, managing their lifecycle and delivering change, drawing on how I've run it at Starship.

AreaWhat I'd put in placeWhy it matters
Requirements & acceptance criteriaOne template for every change: the business need, acceptance criteria and owner, traceable from request to releaseNothing reaches build without a clear definition of done
Product data in the ERPAgreed setup standards for product and item data, with checks before a product goes liveProduct Managers, Licensing and delivery teams can trust what they see
Lifecycle stagesClear entry and exit criteria for each stage, from concept through launch to withdrawalEveryone knows what ready means at each step
Delivery rhythmScrum sprints with a single prioritised backlog in Jira and a short weekly progress viewProgress across several initiatives is visible in one place
Alignment with IT and partner teamsNamed owners, a shared dependency log and a fortnightly check-in with IT and planning teamsBlockers surface early instead of at go-live
Readiness & handoverA readiness checklist and handover pack before a product reaches the teams that sell and deliver itLaunches are predictable and repeatable
